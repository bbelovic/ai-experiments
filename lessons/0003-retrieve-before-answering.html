The evidence path

In retrieval augmented generation (RAG), an application retrieves relevant material and supplies it as context for a model's answer. Spring AI provides components for this pattern, including retrieval advisors. Retrieving a passage does not guarantee the generated answer accurately reflects it.

Research documents -> passages + source metadata
                               |
                          searchable corpus

User question -> retrieve relevant chunks
              -> question + selected evidence sent to model
              -> answer with source references
              -> check whether evidence supports the claims

Here, grounding means supporting answer claims with supplied evidence. A reference is useful only when the cited passage supports the claim. Treat retrieved document text as evidence to examine, not as instructions with authority over the application.

For our project, keep two data paths distinct. The EDGAR and Dividend Watch loaders return structured financial statements, which a tool can supply directly. Narrative questions need a document source: we will start with a small, explicit corpus of research passages. A financial table alone cannot establish why revenue changed.

This lesson is self-contained and does not require the repository's filing parser or chunk classes. A chunk here simply means a piece of document text chosen as a retrieval unit, with metadata identifying its source. We can inspect retrieval manually before adding embeddings or a vector store.

A deliberately small example

The following is synthetic practice data, not a statement about a real company:

Document A, Risk Factors, 2024: "We depend on a small number of suppliers. An interruption at a supplier could delay production."

Question: "Did supplier interruptions reduce revenue in 2024?" Distinguish a described risk from evidence that the event actually occurred.

Try it

1. Which answer is supported by Document A?

2. We ask about supplier risk, retrieve only an unrelated tax paragraph, and get a fluent supplier answer. What should we inspect first?

Evidence drill: choose a passage

Use these two synthetic records. They are teaching examples, not actual company disclosures or output from either loader.

id: DEMO-RISK-2024
company: DEMO
period: 2024
source: demo-annual-report-2024
section: Risk Factors
text: We depend on a small number of suppliers. An interruption
      at a supplier could delay production.

id: DEMO-RESULTS-2024
company: DEMO
period: 2024
source: demo-annual-report-2024
section: Management Discussion
text: Revenue increased during 2024. Management attributes the
      increase to higher sales volumes.
  1. For "What supplier risk did DEMO disclose in 2024?", choose the record you would retrieve and write a one-sentence answer citing its ID.
  2. Identify the metadata that lets a reader locate the source document and section. Distinguish that from company and period metadata used to filter retrieval.
  3. Write a nearby question that neither passage can answer. Explain what additional evidence you would need.
Check your evidence selection after attempting

The supplier-risk question is supported by DEMO-RISK-2024. Its source and section identify where the passage belongs; company and period help narrow the search. DEMO-RESULTS-2024 supports management's stated explanation of revenue growth, but neither passage establishes the amount of revenue lost to supplier interruptions.

Recall without looking above

Explain retrieval versus generation. If the right passage was retrieved but the model invented a claim, which part failed? How is RAG different from MCP?

Self-check after attempting

Retrieval selects evidence; generation constructs an answer using context. Relevant evidence can still be misrepresented during generation. RAG is an application pattern; MCP is a communication protocol. A retrieval capability may be exposed through MCP, and RAG may also be implemented without MCP.
